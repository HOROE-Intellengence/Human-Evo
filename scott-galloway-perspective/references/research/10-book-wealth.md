## 0. TL;DR（先读这五条）

1. **公式在书里根本没有被"论证"过。** `FOCUS + (STOICISM × TIME × DIVERSIFICATION)` 只以**四张章节扉页的排版图形**出现（p.14 / p.54 / p.112 / p.156），全书**没有任何一句话解释为什么 Focus 是加法、其余三项是乘法**。这是本次核实最重要的结论，详见 §2。
2. 全书唯一的硬目标函数：**年 burn（含税）× 25 = 你的"数字"**（4% 规则）。
3. "Rich"这个词他基本不用；他用的是 **economic security**，定义为 *"passive income greater than your burn"*（p.4、p.9 各出现一次，措辞不同，详见 §5）。
4. 职业观的原句是 **"Market dynamics trump individual performance."**（p.74）——**不是** "sector dynamics will trump your talent"。后者是坊间转述，书中无此原文，详见 §6。
5. 他大量自曝反面案例（Red Envelope 亏掉 70% 净资产、Netflix 卖飞、买私人飞机），但**从未披露自己的实际资产配置比例**，详见 §8。

## 1. 完整章节目录 + 每章一句话核心论点

### INTRODUCTION — WEALTH（p.1–13）

| 小节 | 核心论点（一句话） |
|---|---|
| （章首） | 资本主义是"最高产的经济体系，同时是一头贪婪的野兽"，本书讲 what is，不讲 what should be。 |

| Why Wealth? | 财富 = 经济焦虑的缺席；第一课是"security 不取决于你赚多少，而取决于你留下多少 + 知道多少算够"。 |
| Permission Slip | 追求财富不等于不道德；系统需要修，但在修好之前你得先自保。 |
| The Number | 经济安全可归结为一个数字：年 burn × 25。 |
| Two Jackets and a Glove | 童年两件丢失的夹克 + 儿子丢失的手套：经济焦虑是"高血压"，是他一生的驱动力。 |

**INTRODUCTION 一句话**：财富的定义不是收入而是资产，目标是让被动收入超过 burn，公式有四个部件。

### 3. TIME（p.111–154）

| 小节 | 核心论点 |
|---|---|
| （章首） | 时间长期是盟友、短期是敌人；三个面向：复利、当下、未来的自我。 |
| THE POWER OF TIME: COMPOUNDING | 时间把微小变化放大成庞然大物。 |
| Compound Interest | $100 @8% 三十年 → $1,006（无复利只有 $340）。 |
| Inflation | 复利的邪恶双胞胎；美国百年均值约 3%。 |
| Real Returns: Interest vs. Inflation | 只有跑赢通胀的"实际回报"才算数；持现金每年成本 3%（复利）。 |
| NOW | 改变你人生的时间尺度，就改变了你的人生。 |
| Cognitive Errors | 我们系统性地误判时间；机会成本最常被忽略。 |
| Time Is the Real Currency | 私人飞机的算术：换回和儿子的 4 个月。 |
| DO THE MATH | 什么在偷你的时间？社交媒体"可能是史上最大的财富毁灭器之一"。 |
| Youth's Advantage | 年轻人最富有的是时间；经济学家说早期该少存，但**存钱是肌肉**。 |
| What Gets Measured Gets Managed | 每个富人都痴迷于钱；要"理性痴迷"。20/30 代**追踪支出比追踪储蓄更重要**。 |
| Budgeting Your Head Above Water | 建立"waterline budget"（现实最低月支出），让收入高过水线。 |
| Goals | 远期储蓄目标会反噬；设近期、可达成的目标。 |
| Three Buckets | 消费 / 中期 / 长期三个桶。 |
| Allocating Your Buckets | 中期桶管两个变量：流动性与波动性。 |
| The Emergency Fund | 从零开始先攒 $1,000；三到六个月是老教条，实际"看情况"。 |
| Take the Match | 401(k) 雇主匹配必须拿满——即时的、递延税的 100% 回报。 |
| Allocation in Practice | Jack 的样板月度分配（$60,000 年薪）。 |
| Debt | 债务是双刃武器；**债务不该活得比它买的资产更久**。 |
| THE FUTURE | 在当下的你与未来的你之间做平衡。 |
| You Are Not Your Future Self, but You Will Be | projection bias；经济安全是"保留选项"不是"锁死选项"。 |
| Black Swans Will Happen, Meteors Will Strike | 意外必然发生；心理防线和财务防线同等重要。 |
| Planning and Advisers | 25x 是目标不是计划；请 fiduciary（RIA + CFP/CFA）。你付钱买的不是超额收益，是规划、问责与信心。 |

**TIME 一句话**：时间是唯一不可再生的资产，复利与通胀都靠它工作，所以要现在开始、追踪支出、并把储蓄当肌肉练。

## 2. ★ 公式的确切原文与"为什么是加法+乘法"（核心核实项）

### 2.1 公式在书里长什么样

**结论：书里没有把公式写成一行行内文字。** 它是**四张全页扉页排版**，分别位于四章之前（epub 中为独立文件 `16_.xhtml`–`19_.xhtml`，class 为 `x03-CO-Formula`）。纯文本抽取后的形态：

```
FOCUS
+
(STOICISM
×
TIME
×
DIVERSIFICATION)
```

四次出现的页码与被"点亮"的项：

| 扉页 | 页码 | 排版上被突出的项（不带 `_3-CO-Formula` 灰化样式的那一项） |
|---|---|---|
| 第 1 章前 | **p.14** | **STOICISM** |
| 第 2 章前 | **p.54** | **FOCUS** |
| 第 3 章前 | **p.112** | **TIME** |
| 第 4 章前 | **p.156** | **DIVERSIFICATION** |

**【原书明确】** 全书正文中**"FOCUS + (STOICISM × TIME × DIVERSIFICATION)" 这一串符号从未在句子里被引用、命名或解释**。索引里对应条目写作 "basic formula for economic security, 3, 4–5, 152"。

### 2.2 他给这个公式的唯一"文字表述"

最接近公式定义的文字，出现在 Introduction 末尾（p.13），紧接在 "The Hard Way" 一节之后——注意这里**只有一个四项清单，没有任何运算符**：

> **"Executing this plan is not as simple as stating it. That goes beyond finance, beyond what you can capture on a spreadsheet. Wealth is the product of a life well lived—hard work, frugality, wisdom. That doesn't mean being a monk—there's room for pleasure, for error, for life. But it does mean hard work, and it does mean a certain amount of discipline. And it's worth it. The Algebra of Wealth has four components:**
>
> **Stoicism** is about living an intentional, temperate life in and out of work. It's about saving money, for sure, but also developing strong character and connecting with a community. This stuff matters.
>
> **Focus** is primarily about earning an income. As I discuss, income alone won't make you wealthy, but it's the necessary first step. And you're going to need a decent amount of it. So we'll help you plan and navigate a career and maximize the income it generates.
>
> **Time** is your most important asset. It starts and ends with an understanding of the most powerful force in the universe: compound interest. We'll share how to make it work for you. Time is the real currency, the one asset we're all given at birth, and the foundation of wealth.
>
> **Diversification** is our take on the traditional personal finance questions, a road map for making sound investment decisions and for being an educated participant in the financial marketplace.
>
> Okay—let's light this candle."
> — [Introduction / The Hard Way, p.13]

紧接着的下一页（p.14）就是第一张公式扉页。

### 2.3 他对"公式本身"的其他文字锚点

书里唯二可以被当作公式语义注脚的句子：

**(a) 分工式的两句话**（Diversification / Risk, Part Deux，p.165，原书为居中排版的两行）：
> "Focus your time to maximize your current income.
> Diversify your investments to maximize your long-term wealth."

**(b) Epilogue 的四句式复述**（p.258）——这是全书唯一一次把四要素按公式顺序重新说一遍：
> "Find something you're good at that people will pay you for and go hard, really hard, at it. Spend less than you make so you can deploy a platoon, then a division, then an army of capital that fights for you and your loved ones while you sleep. Diversify so you can endure the unknown that surrounds us. And have a long-term perspective: embrace the wisdom to recognize time will go faster than you think."
> — [Epilogue: The Whole Shooting Match, p.258]

**(c) 章节间的相互依赖声明**（Stoicism / Working Hard Does Not Equal Character，p.30）——这是最接近"乘法关系"的表述：
> "None of the advice I offer in the next chapter, 'Focus,' will get you where you need to be unless you can also follow the spending and saving advice in the chapter after that, 'Time.'"

### 2.4 判定

| 问题 | 结论 |
|---|---|
| 公式原文写法是否为 `FOCUS + (STOICISM × TIME × DIVERSIFICATION)`？ | ✅ 是。四次出现，写法完全一致（含括号）。 |
| 书里有没有"为什么是加法+乘法"的解释原话？ | ❌ **没有。全书零解释。** |
| 有没有任何一句话说"某一项为零则全归零"？ | ❌ 没有。全书 `zero` 一词共 10 处，无一处用于解释公式。 |
| 有没有说 Focus 与另外三项是不同性质的？ | 🟡 只有间接线索：Introduction 说 Focus 是 "the necessary first step"、"income alone won't make you wealthy"；Epilogue 与 p.165 把 Focus（赚当期收入）与 Diversification（长期财富）分列。 |

**【归纳】** 排版的编辑意图大概率是：Focus（收入）是被加进来的**原材料/基数**，另外三项是**作用在它上面的乘数**——Stoicism 决定有多少比例能留下来、Time 决定复利跑多少年、Diversification 决定这条曲线会不会被一次归零打断。但这是从上下文推出来的，**不是 Galloway 写的**。任何把这套解释当作"他说过"的引用都属于二次创作。引用时必须标注为推论。

## 5. ★ "Rich is having passive income greater than your burn" 的确切上下文

**核实结果：书里没有这一句字面表述。** 这是 Galloway 在播客/newsletter 里常用的口语版本。书中对应的表述有**两处**，措辞不同，且他用的词是 **economic security / wealth**，不是 "rich"。

### 出处 1（完整定义，Introduction / Why Wealth?，p.4）

> "My objective for you is straightforward. **Economic security is acquiring sufficient assets—not income, but assets—such that the passive income they generate exceeds the level of spending you choose for yourself—your burn rate.** Passive income is money your money makes: interest you make on money you loan to someone else, appreciation in the value of your real estate, dividends paid by stocks you hold, rent paid by the tenant of an apartment you own. I'll talk more about these and other sources of passive income later, but, in short, it is any income that's not compensation paid for working at a job. And your burn rate is how much you spend, day to day and month to month. **If your passive income is greater than your burn, you don't need to work (though you may want to), because you don't need the compensation to pay your expenses.**
>
> **That's wealth.** There are numerous paths to it; the reliable ones take time and hard work but are within the grasp of most people. And reaching for it should be a priority, early. Economic security is control. It is knowing that you can plan for the future, commit your time as you see fit, and provide for those who depend on you."

**上下文**：这段紧接在两句定义之后——
> "Wealth is a means to an end: economic security. Put another way, **wealth is the absence of economic anxiety.**"（p.4）
> "This is the first lesson in this book: **economic security isn't a function of what you earn but what you keep and knowing how much is enough for you.**"（p.4）

### 出处 2（压缩版，Introduction / The Number，p.9）

> "Thomas J. Stanley said, 'Wealth is not a matter of intelligence, it's a matter of arithmetic.' **Remember our math: passive income greater than your burn.**"

### 出处 3（作为"数字"的终点，Diversification，p.197）

> "Continue adding capital to your investment account until you've hit your number (i.e., it's enough that you can live off your passive income alone)."

**引用建议**：如果要写成一句话，最安全的逐字引用是 **"Remember our math: passive income greater than your burn."**（p.9）；若要完整定义，用 p.4 那段。**不要**把 "Rich is…" 当作书中原文引用。

## 6. ★ 职业选择论述（含"sector dynamics"核实）

### 6.1 "Market dynamics trump individual performance" —— 原始出处段落

**核实结果：书中原文是 "Market dynamics trump individual performance"，不是 "sector dynamics will trump your talent"。** 全书 "trump" 一词只出现两次，均为此句（正文 + 章末回顾）。

**正文（Focus / Some Basics Re: Careers，p.74）**：
> "**Market dynamics trump individual performance.** (I realize how awful that sounds.) Someone of average talent at Google has done better over the past decade than someone great at General Motors. Especially early in your career, be thoughtful about the wave you are paddling into; any opportunity you have when you're young to choose among different paths is a profound blessing.
>
> **Look for the best beach with the biggest waves.** Twenty-five years ago, I chose the e-commerce wave. My first effort (Red Envelope) failed. Even worse, it failed slowly . . . over ten years (see below: 'Know When to Quit'). But I'd picked the right wave. I paddled back out and started a firm (L2) that helped other firms develop digital strategies. It took me a while, but the strength and size of the wave kept me moving, carried me forward, and gave people the impression I was a more talented surfer than I am. I was just riding a Nazaré-size wave."

**章末回顾（Focus / Chapter Review，p.110）**：
> "◾ **Look for the beach with the biggest waves.** Market dynamics trump individual performance, so give yourself the best chance by going where the opportunities are the greatest."

### 6.2 "Follow your talent, not your passion" —— 原始出处段落

**开篇（Focus / Don't Follow Your Passion，p.60）**：
> "If someone tells you to follow your passion, it means they're already rich. And typically, they made their fortune in some unglamorous industry like iron ore smelting. Your mission is to find something you're good at and apply the thousands of hours of grit and sacrifice necessary to become great at it. As you get there, the feeling of growth and your increasing mastery of your craft, along with the economic rewards, recognition, and camaraderie, will make you passionate about whatever 'it' is."

**为什么 passion 不可执行（p.61）**：
> "Stanford psychologist William Damon found that only 20% of people younger than 26 can articulate a passion that guides their life choices. So four out of five of us can't follow our passion even if we want to, because we don't know what it is. And even when we can 'articulate a passion,' it's often socially determined… For most of us the kind of passion that guides us, a lodestar-on-the-horizon kind of passion, is not a birthright. It's something we find through hard work."

**Steve Jobs 的反证（p.61）**：
> "Jobs didn't find what he loved, he found his talent. He became passionate about marketing consumer computers—what he would later call 'bicycles for the mind'—because he was so good at it."

**为什么 talent 优于 passion（p.63）**：
> "In contrast with passion, talent is observable and testable; it can be more readily converted to a high-earning career, and it gets better the more you exploit it. Passion for something might make you better at it—talent absolutely will. Economists refer to 'match quality' as the fit between a worker's talents and the job. And studies repeatedly show that people perform better, improve faster, and make more money where they have high match quality."

**收尾（p.70）**：
> "Achieve economic security and follow your passion on weekends."

**章末回顾（p.110）**：
> "◾ **Don't follow your passion. Follow your talent.**"

### 6.3 行业动能 / 宏观时机

**衰退期创业（Focus，p.75）**：
> "Economic downturns are, in my view, the best time to start a business. I've started nine companies, and the only factor I can identify across the successful ones is that I started them in recessions. It's not just me. Microsoft was founded in a mid-seventies recession, and Apple just after it ended. The post-2008 Great Recession produced Airbnb, Uber, Slack, WhatsApp, and Block. … In downturns, good salaried jobs are hard to find, because nobody is quitting. So good people (and cheap assets) are plentiful. The absence of cheap, easy capital means the concept has to work from day one. Founders in downturns imprint a more disciplined DNA on the company's culture—they have no choice."

**要"薪酬能随利润/估值放大"的岗位（p.73）**：
> "Speaking of upside potential, some industries scale better than others, as does compensation. You are looking for roles where compensation scales with profits or valuation increases. … Even if the industry is scalable, your compensation only scales if it's tied to profits, either through a bonus plan or equity. In sum, you want a piece of the action."

**热情行业 = 过度投资 → 低 ROI（Media 一节，p.83–84）**：
> "Jobs in sexy industries offer lower ROI on your efforts, as they are overinvested, which drives down returns. You don't want to do the weather at 3:00 a.m. on Sunday? Okay, we'll find a dozen other people who think this is their path to anchoring the evening news."

**"Zig When Others Zag"（Diversification，p.251）**：
> "When everyone is barking up the same tree, we get stupid and lose money. Zig when everyone else is zagging. Initially, money pouring into a sector can create a market… But soon, the more money, the greater the entry price and the lower the return. … With student debt, the more you pay, the less your degree is worth."

**杠杆政府投资（p.74–75）**：
> "Extreme wealth creation is easier for a firm that leverages government investment or fallow assets. … Silicon Valley is fundamentally the most successful government investment in history. … You pay taxes, so those investments are yours for the leveraging."

## 9. ★ 风险与局限：他自己承认这套方法在什么情况下不适用

### 9.1 明确的适用边界声明

**(a) 不适用于已经财务失控的人（Introduction，p.3）**：
> "In sum, I'm not trying to beat Suze Orman at her own game—and **if you've got bill collectors coming after you, start with her. This book is for those who have their act together** and want to ensure they make the best of their blessings."

**(b) 不要以异类为模板（Introduction，p.2）**：
> "**My first piece of advice is you should assume you are not Jay-Z, Ronald Read, or Warren Buffett.** Each was an outlier, not just in talent but in good fortune."

**(c) 只适用于美国税制（Diversification，p.238）**：
> "**My experience and understanding is mainly in the U.S., so that's what I'll focus on here**, but many of these issues are universal."
> 以及 p.227："**Tax laws change often, so look into how the current rules apply to you before you buy a home.**"

**(d) 视角被知识工作者经验染色（Focus / Main Street Economy，p.91）**：
> "**Most of my examples and advice (including the next section about getting to a city) are colored by my lived experience in the knowledge worker fields.** But the core message of this book, and the path to wealth, can apply to any career path, and the Main Street economy is an economic engine for millions of Americans. Don't overlook it."

**(e) 他不懂所有职业（Focus，p.77）**：
> "**I don't pretend to know what translates to success in every career**, and you need to dig into the critical success factors for any career you are considering."

**(f) 他的金融专业度不均匀（Diversification，p.171）**：
> "**My expertise in these areas varies broadly, as does anyone else's**—no single person has an expert-level understanding of the entirety of our economy."

### 9.3 他对"系统本身"的承认

> "Seeking wealth is not always in fashion. In a society correctly concerned about the acceleration of income inequality, wealth looks like the unfair allocation of a rigged system. 'Every billionaire is a policy failure.' Maybe. Or maybe not. But it's not relevant here. **The urgent issue you face is your own economic security, not the virtues of anyone else's.**" — [Introduction / Permission Slip, p.5]

> "The system may need fixing; until then, you have to stand it. … **None of this is your fault. Society faces greater risks than you becoming a millionaire.**" — [Introduction / Permission Slip, p.7]

> "**Ignoring the role of luck is just as dangerous for the unsuccessful. This is the pernicious downside of our 'you can do anything you put your mind to' ethos. Because the implication is that if you don't succeed, it must be your fault.**" — [Stoicism / Luck, p.35]

**【归纳】** 这是全书方法论上最诚实、也最少被引用的一层：他把"这套方法可能对你不管用"归因于运气与出生条件，而不是意志力；同时又坚持"在系统被修好之前，你只能自保"。这两句话构成了他整本书的道德许可（permission slip）——也是他能同时抨击资本主义和教人在资本主义里赢的自洽点。
