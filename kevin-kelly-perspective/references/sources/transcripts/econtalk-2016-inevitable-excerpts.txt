ROBERTS CHALLENGES KELLY ON "TRANSFORMATIVE" TECHNOLOGY:
Roberts, pushing back on how much of what Kelly calls "inevitable" technology
actually changes daily life:
"...these are not *transformative* applications..."
(referring to examples like thermostats and lane-detection systems)

ROBERTS ON THE BOOK'S GEOGRAPHIC BIAS:
"Your book is a little bit San-Francisco-centric...Is it really true that
most or all of us can do these glorious things?"

ROBERTS ON ATTENTION AND INTROSPECTION:
"I worry about contemplation. I worry about introspection. We're so
bombarded...by *stuff*."

ROBERTS INVOKING HAYEKIAN LIMITS ON KELLY'S DETERMINISM:
"...you can't repeal the laws of economics."

KELLY'S RESPONSES:

On invisible technology being the marker of success:
"...it succeeds to the extent that it transforms while we don't see it."

On job displacement, reframed as a generative process rather than a loss:
"Our jobs into the future will be to invent jobs that we can automate."

On humans retaining a durable premium over automation:
"...we will prefer, and therefore we will *pay more* to be around humans."

On the limits of his own "inevitability" thesis — this is the line most
useful for showing Kelly is not a pure determinist:
"...we don't necessarily have to do everything technology wants."

BIOLOGICAL/EVOLUTIONARY FRAME (paraphrase, not exact quote): Kelly
distinguishes "macro forms" that are inevitable (the way four-legged
quadrupeds are a stable large-scale outcome of evolution) from the specific
"species" or implementations, which remain unpredictable — this is his
standing answer to "aren't you a technological determinist?"

ANALYST NOTE: Of the four EconTalk appearances (2007, 2010, 2013, 2016), this
is the most confrontational and highest-density exchange, and Russ Roberts'
description of Kelly elsewhere in the same conversational tradition — "you are
not a salesman, you confront the costs" (2010 episode) — is a useful outside
characterization of Kelly's argumentative style: he concedes specific costs
readily but almost never concedes the larger thesis.
