## "The Nine Laws of God" — Out of Control (1994), Chapter 24
URL: https://kk.org/mt-files/outofcontrol/ch24-a.html
Opening (verbatim): "Out of nothing, nature makes something. First there is hard rock planet; then is life, lots of it. First barren hills; then brooks with fish and cattails and red-winged blackbirds." ... "First a hunk of metal; then a robot. First some wires; then a mind."
(Note: parallel "First X; then Y" anaphora repeated 4x — a clear structural fingerprint: nature-to-technology analogy delivered via repeated grammatical parallelism.)

## "1,000 True Fans" (2008 essay, later revised)
Mirror used: https://vilidatsov.wordpress.com/2017/08/17/1000-true-fans-by-kevin-kelly/
Opening two paragraphs (verbatim):
"To be a successful creator you don't need millions. You don't need millions of dollars or millions of customers, millions of clients or millions of fans. To make a living as a craftsperson, photographer, musician, designer, author, animator, app maker, entrepreneur, or inventor you need only thousands of true fans."

"A true fan is defined as a fan that will buy anything you produce. These diehard fans will drive 200 miles to see you sing; they will buy the hardback and paperback and audible versions of your book; they will purchase your next figurine sight unseen; they will pay for the 'best-of' DVD version of your free youtube channel; they will come to your chef's table once a month."

(Note: anaphora device — "they will... they will... they will... they will..." — repeated parallel clauses building a cumulative, almost incantatory rhythm. Also note the definitional opening move: state the counterintuitive thesis first ("you don't need millions"), THEN build the case — consistent with "conclusion-first" rhetorical pattern.)
